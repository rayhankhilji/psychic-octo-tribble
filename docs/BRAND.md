# TinyTown brand book

How TinyTown looks, sounds and moves, and where the files are. The kit lives in `public/brand/` (served at `/brand/`, with a press page) and is regenerated by `tools/brand-assets.mjs`. When this page and the code disagree, the code wins: `src/ui/brand.ts` (the mark, `BRAND` colours) and the tokens at the top of `src/ui/styles.css`.

## 1. The idea

**TinyTown is a Tamagotchi for your startup:** a tiny, sunny San Francisco Bay that grows with your real MRR, customers and releases.

The brand is that city: **sunny, honest, small and well made.**

- **Sunny.** Warm sand paper, Bay blue, Golden Gate orange, a late-afternoon sun. Never cold tech blue, never neon.
- **Honest.** The city never fakes a number, and neither do we. Every claim quotes the real figure behind it. The demo is always labelled *Nightjar, a demo startup*.
- **Small.** A toy-sized world you could hold. Isometric, tidy and detailed close up.
- **Well made.** It should feel like a premium indie game with Linear/Stripe-level product polish. That means craft over decoration: one beautiful render beats five effects.
- **A little wry.** Warm and dry, never jokey. *"This street isn't on the map."*

## 2. Logo

### The mark

An isometric city block on a sand tile: Bay-blue sides, a Golden Gate–orange tower with window bands, two cream blocks with a roof garden, a lawn with one tree, and the sun coming up behind it. The geometry is in `src/ui/brand.ts` (`markParts()`, a 48 × 48 box). **Don't redraw it.** Every file in the kit is generated from that code, so the mark is identical everywhere.

### The wordmark

"TinyTown" in **Bricolage Grotesque ExtraBold** (weight 800), **88% width**, **44 pt optical size**, **−0.03 em tracking**. One word with two capitals. It's never "Tiny Town", "TINYTOWN" or "Tinytown". The all-caps TINYTOWN is the **sign on Twin Peaks**, a thing in the world. It isn't the logo. Lockup files use outlines (`tools/brand/wordmark.json`), so they need no font installed.

In the app's top bar the wordmark is live text (`.wordmark`: 800, 90% width, −0.03 em) next to a 34 px mark. That's fine at UI sizes. Everywhere else, use the files.

### Lockups

| Lockup | Use it for | Files |
|---|---|---|
| **Horizontal** (mark + word) | The default: site headers, decks, partner pages, email | `logo-horizontal-light.svg` / `-dark.svg` (+ 1024 px PNGs) |
| **Stacked** (word under the mark) | Square or centred spaces: the boot screen, covers, merch | `logo-stacked-light.svg` / `-dark.svg` (+ 1024 px PNGs) |
| **Mark only** | Avatars, app icons, favicons, small spaces, watermarks | `mark.svg`, `mark-512.png`, `mark-1024.png` |
| **Mark on a tile** | App icons, the Stripe App listing, anywhere that wants a square | `mark-tile-sand.svg`, `mark-tile-ink.svg` (+ 512 px PNGs) |
| **One colour** | Stamps, embossing, single-colour print, laser-etching | `mark-mono-ink.svg`, `mark-mono-cream.svg` (+ 512 px PNGs) |

`-light` files have ink text, for light backgrounds. `-dark` files have cream text, for dark backgrounds. The mark itself works on both, so it has one full-colour file.

Proportions (in the mark's 48-unit box): the horizontal word is set at 34 units, so its cap height is about half the mark's drawn height, with the cap band centred just below the mark's middle where the tile's weight sits. The gap is 9 units. The stacked word is 23.5 units, 7 units under the tile, centred on the tile, not on the sun.

### Clear space

Keep clear space on every side equal to **the height of the "T"** in the wordmark (its cap height). For the mark alone, use **a quarter of the mark's width**. Nothing goes inside that space: no text, no edges, no other logos.

### Minimum sizes

| Lockup | Screen | Print |
|---|---|---|
| Horizontal | 24 px tall (about 97 px wide) | 6 mm tall |
| Stacked | 48 px tall | 12 mm tall |
| Mark | 16 px (favicon) | 5 mm |
| One-colour mark | 32 px: the hairline gaps close up below that | 8 mm |

Below 24 px tall, use the mark alone.

### On light, dark and photos

- **Light** (sand, cream, white): `-light` files.
- **Dark** (ink `#1b2236`, night `#0f1424`): `-dark` files. The mark stays full colour; its sand tile carries it.
- **On the city or a photo:** put the logo on calm space, such as sky, open water or a paper wash (see the social templates), never over busy streets. If you can't find calm space, put it on a sand or ink panel.
- **On Golden Gate orange or Bay blue:** use the one-colour cream mark and cream text. The full-colour mark disappears into its own colours.

### Don'ts, and why

- **Don't recolour the mark, or swap its faces.** The light falls from the top left: top faces lightest, left faces mid, right faces darkest. Recolouring breaks the 3D.
- **Don't add effects:** no outer glows, bevels, gradients or long shadows. A soft drop shadow under the mark (as on the boot screen and 404) is the only one, because the block is sitting on paper.
- **Don't stretch, skew or rotate it.** It's isometric on purpose. Tilted further, it stops being a block.
- **Don't set the wordmark in another font, or retype it in Bricolage at a different width or tracking.** Use the files.
- **Don't rearrange the lockups:** no word to the left of the mark, no new spacing, no taglines locked into the logo.
- **Don't put the mark in a circle or a badge** (platforms crop the avatars themselves) or on a purple gradient.
- **Don't use emoji next to it**, or anywhere else in the brand.

## 3. Colour

### The palette

| Name | Hex | Role |

| Gate text | `#a83818` | Orange for small text on sand (5.5:1). |
| Gate light | `#ff7a50` / `#ff8a5c` | Orange on dark backgrounds; the tower's top face. |
| **Bay blue** | `#2c6fb7` | Information: links, focus rings, charts that aren't money, water. The tile's sides. |
| Bay text | `#235e9c` | Blue for small text on sand (5.7:1). Bay deep `#1d4f8a`. |
| **Sun** | `#f6b53d` | Highlights only: a milestone, the sun in the mark, the clock. Never text on light backgrounds (1.5:1). |
| **Sand** | `#f5ecdb` | The paper: the day background. Lighter surfaces `#fdf9f2`, `#f8f1e4`; deeper `#e9d9bb`. |
| **Ink** | `#1b2236` | Text by day; the deep surface. Secondary `#4b5168`, quietest `#62667a`. |
| **Night** | `#0f1424` | The night background. Surfaces `#161c2f`, `#1d2439`. |
| **Cream** | `#f6eedf` | Text by night, and the `-dark` wordmark. Secondary `#c8c2b6`. |
| Good / bad | `#24774c` / `#c8382b` | Status only: growth, churn, errors. Never decoration. |

In code, use the tokens (`--accent`, `--bay`, `--ink`, `--bg`…), never raw hex. `BRAND` in `brand.ts` is the source for canvas and SVG.

### Day and night

The product has two themes because the city has two moods: **day** (sand paper, ink text) and **night** (ink-navy paper, cream text, a brighter orange `#ff6a3d` for the accent). The night theme isn't an inverted day theme. It's the same city after sunset. Marketing follows the image: a day render gets day type, a night render gets night type.

### Ratios of use

Roughly **70% sand or night** (paper and the city), **20% ink or cream** (type), **under 10% orange**, and a touch of blue and sun. Orange is precious: one orange thing per view. If everything is orange, nothing is the button.

### Contrast rules

Body text meets **4.5:1**, large display text (24 px+) **3:1**. Measured:

| Pair | Ratio | OK for |
|---|---|---|
| Ink on sand | 13.5 | everything |
| Ink-3 `#62667a` on sand | 4.8 | the quietest text allowed |
| Gate text `#a83818` on sand | 5.5 | small orange text |
| Golden Gate `#e5512b` on sand | 3.2 | display type and icons only (a headline's one orange word) |
| White on gate fill `#cc4420` | 4.8 | button labels |
| White on `#e5512b` | 3.8 | **not** for labels, so buttons use the fill |
| Bay text `#235e9c` on sand | 5.7 | links |
| Cream on night | 15.9 | everything |
| Orange `#ff7a50` on night | 7.1 | text and accents at night |

## 4. Type

| Face | Role | Settings |
|---|---|---|
| **Bricolage Grotesque** | Display: the wordmark, headlines, dialog titles, the hero numbers | 750–800, 88–95% width, −0.03 to −0.035 em, line-height 0.98–1.1 |
| **Geist** | UI and body: labels, buttons, paragraphs | 400–650, line-height 1.45 (UI) to 1.6 (reading) |
| **Geist Mono** | Codes, keys, IDs, and editorial eyebrows and captions (the 404's "404 · NOTHING BUILT HERE", the social captions) | 500, caps, +0.06 to +0.1 em |

All three load from Google Fonts in `index.html`. Don't add a fourth.

**The UI scale** (`styles.css`): `--t-xs` 11 · `--t-sm` 12 · `--t-md` 13.5 (body) · `--t-lg` 15 · `--t-xl` 20 · `--t-2xl` 28 (hero numbers). Marketing goes bigger with the same faces: 44 px headline on the 404 and boot screen, 58–80 px on social images.

**Rules.**

- **Tight display tracking.** Bricolage at −0.03 to −0.035 em, narrowed to 88–95%. Body Geist at 0 to −0.01 em. Never track out lowercase.
- **Tabular figures for money and counts** (`.num`, `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`), so a ticking MRR doesn't wobble.
- **Sentence case** everywhere: buttons, titles, menus ("Go to a place", "Manage billing"). Capitals are for names (Stripe, TinyTown Hills) and the sign.
- **Eyebrows in caps** with wide tracking: Geist 600 at 10.5 px in dense UI (`.eyebrow`), Geist Mono 500 in editorial places.
- One display line per view. Use `text-wrap: balance` for headlines and `pretty` for paragraphs, and a non-breaking space to keep the last two words together ("San&nbsp;Francisco").

## 5. Imagery and icons

- **The city is the hero image.** Use real renders from the game (`tools/brand-assets.mjs social`, or `tools/shot.mjs`): isometric, tilt-shifted, at a real time of day. Golden hour (`time=18:30`) is the signature look; midday is the clearest; night is for drama.
- **Label the demo.** Any image of the demo city says *Nightjar, a demo startup* somewhere, with its real numbers.
- **No stock photos, no AI-generated cities, no mock-ups of fake dashboards.** If it isn't in the game, don't draw it.
- **Line art:** the one-line skyline (the Golden Gate in orange, downtown and the Transamerica Pyramid in ink, Twin Peaks with the sun) on the boot screen and the 404. Round caps and joins, 1.2–1.6 px strokes, ink plus at most two brand colours. Use it for empty states and small moments, not as decoration on every page.
- **UI icons** are the hand-drawn line set in `src/ui/icons.ts`: a 20-unit grid, 1.6 strokes, round caps and joins, `currentColor`. Never emoji, never a mixed icon font.

## 6. Motion

Motion explains. It never decorates.

- **Things enter from where they come from and leave toward where they go.** A panel slides in from its own edge; a toast lands where it will sit; a building rises from its plot.
- **Durations from tokens:** `--dur-1` 120 ms (hover, press) · `--dur-2` 200 ms (small state) · `--dur-3` 320 ms (panels, dialogs) · `--dur-4` 560 ms (scene-level).
- **Easing:** `--ease-out` for entrances, `--ease-in-out` for moves, `--ease-spring` sparingly for delight (a toast landing, a number settling).
- **Stagger groups at 40–70 ms**, and keep a whole sequence under ~900 ms. Nobody should wait on an animation to act.
- **Transform and opacity only** (plus clip-path and stroke-dashoffset for line art). 60 fps on a mid-range laptop with the city running behind.
- **No idle loops on UI chrome.** The city is alive; the interface is calm.
- **`prefers-reduced-motion`:** cut to the end state, or a short fade.

The mark has one animation, on the boot screen: the tile is already there, the tower rises from it (250 ms in), then the blocks (400 ms) on a springy ease, and the sun comes up last (600 ms). The `layer` on each part in `brand.ts` (0 tile … 3 sun) exists for this.

## 7. Voice

Plain, warm, concrete, a little wry. British/US-neutral. We sound like a good shopkeeper who also tracks their numbers.

**Principles, with lines from the product.**

| Principle | Do (real copy) | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| **Quote the real number.** | "Each needs $0.20 of MRR to be housed: 1,000 users need $200. Short of that, people end up in tents." | "Keep your users happy to unlock housing!" |
| **Say what happens, in order.** | "Buildings cost upkeep. Short of it, the TINYTOWN sign goes dark first, then lamps fail, the bridge rusts and the train stops." | "Neglect your city and bad things happen." |
| **Reassure with facts, not adjectives.** | "Checkout closed before paying. Nothing was charged." | "Oops! Something went wrong 😅" |
| **Small and concrete beats grand.** | "Bunk beds, whiteboards, too many cofounders." · "A little house with a yard and a lemon tree." | "A premium living experience for ambitious founders." |
| **Dry, not jokey.** | "This street isn't on the map." · "The pointy one. Every skyline needs one." · "Big domes, bigger questions." | "Uh-oh, you broke the internet!" |
| **State the rule once, plainly.** | "Nothing in the game is for sale. The only way to grow it is to grow your startup." | "Level up faster with Gems!" |
| **Tell people what to do next.** | "Your city and everything you built are safe. It just needs Stripe again to keep up with your revenue." | "Error: integration unavailable." |

**Mechanics.** Sentence case. Numbers as numerals with real separators ($10,000; 1,000 users). "MRR" not "Mrr". Curly quotes and apostrophes in UI copy. No exclamation marks except for a real celebration (and even then, rarely). No "simply", "just" as filler, "unleash", "supercharge", "seamless" or "revolutionary".

**Taglines and headlines** (all true, use as they are):

- *A Tamagotchi for your startup.* (the line)
- *Your MRR builds a tiny San Francisco.*
- *Every $1 of MRR is $10,000 to spend.*
- *The only way to grow the city is to grow your startup.*
- *From a hacker house to a house on the moon.*

### Regenerating the kit

`tools/brand-assets.mjs` rebuilds everything from the code and the live game. Templates for the social images are plain HTML in `tools/brand/social/` (shared styles in `common.css`; `{{city}}`, `{{mrr}}`, `{{customers}}` and `{{day}}` are filled in by the script).

```bash
# Logos, avatars and PNGs (fast). Run after any change to src/ui/brand.ts.
node tools/brand-assets.mjs logos

# Social images: renders the grown demo city, then sets the type over it.
# Use a frozen build so hot reloads can't land mid-render:
npx vite build --outDir /tmp/tt-dist && npx vite preview --outDir /tmp/tt-dist --port 5220 --strictPort &
node tools/brand-assets.mjs social --base=http://localhost:5220
node tools/brand-assets.mjs social --base=http://localhost:5220 --only=ph-4-hills   # one image
node tools/brand-assets.mjs social --reuse    # keep the cached renders, re-set the type only

# The wordmark outlines (only if the wordmark spec changes): needs the variable TTF and fontkit.
npm i --no-save fontkit
node tools/brand-assets.mjs wordmark --font="BricolageGrotesque[opsz,wdth,wght].ttf"
```

Options: `--scale=1.5` renders the city larger than the image for crisp edges; `--cache=<dir>` keeps renders (default: your temp folder); `--settle=9000` is how long the city runs before the shot. Camera framings and times live in the `SOCIAL` list at the top of the script (`view=x,z,zoom`, see DEV_WORKFLOW). Social images are saved as JPEG (quality 90, 4:4:4) when ImageMagick is installed, PNG otherwise. Software WebGL takes one to four minutes per render, so the full set takes a while.
