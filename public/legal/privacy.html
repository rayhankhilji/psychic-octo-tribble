Privacy Policy

Effective [EFFECTIVE DATE] · Last updated 13 October 2026

TinyTown draws a city from your real business numbers, so we know you’re trusting us with something that matters. This policy explains what we collect, why, who helps us, how long we keep it, and how you stay in control.

The short version

  • We collect what we need to run your city: your email, your plan, and daily totals from the accounts you connect.
  • From Stripe and GitHub we read counts and totals. We don’t store your customers’ names, emails or payment details, and we never read your code.
  • Stripe takes your payment. We never see your card number.
  • No ads, no tracking cookies, and we never sell your data.
  • You can download everything we hold about you, or delete your account, from Account in the app.

2What we collect

Your account

Your email address. If you sign in with Google or GitHub, the basic profile they share with us: your email, name, profile picture and an account identifier. We also keep the name you give your city, its layout seed, and when you joined and last visited.

Your plan

Stripe processes your payment. Card details go straight to Stripe; we never see or store them. From Stripe we keep your customer and subscription IDs, your plan, its status and when it renews or ends. Stripe’s own privacy policy covers what Stripe collects.

The accounts you connect

  • Stripe, through our read-only Stripe App. We read your subscriptions and prices to work out, for each day, your MRR and how many paying customers you have. We store your Stripe account ID and name, whether it’s in live or test mode, and an encrypted token that lets us keep reading. Your customers’ records, which can include their names or emails, pass through our server in memory while we add things up. We don’t store them.
  • GitHub, through our read-only GitHub App. For the one repository you pick, we read the dates of commits on its default branch and the names and dates of its releases. We store the installation ID, the repository name and the daily totals. Commit author details are processed in memory and not stored. We don’t read or store your source code.
  • PostHog, if you choose it. We store your personal API key, encrypted, and use it only to read your monthly active-user count.

What comes out is your timeline: one row a day of totals (MRR, customers, active users, releases, commits). That’s what we cache and what your city is drawn from.

Your city

What you’ve built, where, and which home you’ve chosen.

The waitlist

If you joined the waitlist: your email address, when you joined, and whether we’ve invited you.

Emails we send

Sign-in links and messages about your account: your plan starting or ending, a payment that didn’t go through, a connection that stopped working, an invitation from the waitlist. We keep a record of whether each was delivered. We don’t send marketing email unless you’ve asked for it, and any such email has an unsubscribe link.

Product analytics

A handful of milestones, sent from our server: signed up, started checkout, subscribed, connected an account, built something for the first time, cancelled, deleted the account. They’re linked to an internal account ID, not your email. No analytics script runs in your browser.

Error and performance reports

When something breaks in your browser, it sends us the error message, where in our code it happened, the page path (without anything after the ?), and the app version. Once a visit, it also sends how long the city took to load and its frame rate. We strip email addresses and tokens before anything is sent, and these reports aren’t linked to your account.

Security records

Like any website, our servers see your IP address, browser and the time of each request. We use these briefly to limit abuse (rate limiting), keep accounts safe and fix problems. When you sign in by email, Cloudflare Turnstile checks that you’re not a bot, which means Cloudflare sees your IP address and some browser signals.

When you write to us

Whatever you choose to tell us, and our replies.

5International transfers

Most of our providers process data in the United States. When your data leaves the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, we rely on the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses, the UK’s International Data Transfer Addendum, or the EU–US Data Privacy Framework where the provider is certified under it.

6How long we keep it

  • Your account, city, connections and timeline: while your account is open. When you delete your account, they’re removed from our live database straight away and from backups within 30 days.
  • A connection you remove: its token and details are deleted straight away, and your timeline is rebuilt without it.
  • Billing records: as long as tax and accounting law requires, which is often several years. Stripe keeps its own records under its policy.
  • The waitlist: until you sign up, until you ask us to remove you, or 12 months after TinyTown opens to everyone, whichever comes first.
  • Error reports: 90 days. Security records: up to 30 days. Email delivery records: up to 90 days.
  • Product analytics: up to 24 months. Once your account is deleted, the internal ID no longer leads back to you.

7Your choices and rights

In the app, any time

  • Download my data in Account gives you a file of everything we hold about you.
  • Delete account in Account cancels your plan, disconnects everything and deletes your data.
  • Disconnect Stripe, GitHub or PostHog in TinyTown, or uninstall our apps from your Stripe Dashboard or GitHub settings.

Your legal rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, delete or export your data, to restrict or object to how we use it, and to withdraw consent you’ve given. If you’re in the EEA or the UK, you can also complain to your data protection authority (in the UK, the Information Commissioner’s Office), though we’d like the chance to put things right first.

If you’re in California or another US state with a privacy law, you have the right to know what we collect, to have it deleted or corrected, and not to be treated differently for using these rights. We don’t sell personal information or share it for cross-context behavioural advertising.

To use any of these rights, email [SUPPORT EMAIL] from your account’s email address. We’ll reply within 30 days and may need to check it’s really you.

8How we protect it

Everything travels over HTTPS. The tokens and keys that let us read your connected accounts are encrypted with AES-256-GCM, under a key that only our server holds. Our database can’t be reached from a browser, only by our server. We ask for the narrowest read-only permissions each service offers. No system is perfectly secure, but if something goes wrong we’ll tell you and the authorities as the law requires.
